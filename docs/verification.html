A buyer asking "how do I know item #1247 is actually in the collection?" deserves a real answer. The answer cannot be a single percentage. The Sternberg Collection contains 2,468 items spanning fifty-three years, six continents, multiple languages, and dozens of production formats. A single verification metric flattens that complexity into a number that doesn't tell the buyer what matters.

What matters is provenance. Chain of custody. The link between the inventory line and the physical object. The three-tier framework recognizes that different parts of the collection warrant different verification depths, and that all 2,468 items can be brought to documented chain-of-custody status — even items whose bottom stamps are in Hebrew, Japanese, or Thai.

The Sternberg Collection has always been at 100% chain-of-custody. Chaim's color-coded box system, his 9,872-photograph archive, and his 2,466-post Instagram account are the proof. The verification work now is making that chain visible and queryable for a buyer.

The methodology in detail

Tier 1 OCR-verified means the bottom-stamp text has been extracted from a photograph and confirmed against the inventory ticket. This is the strongest single-piece evidence: SKU codes, copyright years, country of origin, and original retail markings are all direct OCR matches. The Sternberg Collection currently has approximately 200 items at Tier 1, concentrated in anchors and high-value pieces where individual verification economics justify the OCR effort.

Tier 2 photo-documented means a photograph in the archive matches the inventory line by side art, distinctive shape, or visible identifier other than bottom-stamp OCR. This is the appropriate verification tier for items with non-English bottom text (Hebrew Jerusalem CMCS, Japanese Roppongi Hills LE, Thai Bahrain CMCS) and items where the bottom is occluded by packaging. Tier 2 work is currently underway across approximately 700-900 items, with capacity to scale to 1,500-2,000 items.

Tier 3 inventory cross-referenced is the provenance-level verification: every inventory line linked to its box location, at least one matching photo file, an Instagram post URL where the piece appears in the @starbucks.mug.collection archive, and a series classification. This is the form of documentation a museum or auction house needs. It is mechanical, not interpretive — the master cross-reference spreadsheet, when complete, IS the verification artifact.
