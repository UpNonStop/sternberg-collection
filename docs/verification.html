The answer cannot be a single percentage. The Sternberg Collection contains 2,468 items spanning fifty-three years, six continents, multiple languages, and dozens of production formats.

A single verification metric flattens that complexity into a number that doesn’t tell the buyer what matters. What matters is provenance. Chain of custody. The link between the inventory line and the physical object. The three-tier framework recognizes that different parts of the collection warrant different verification depths, and that all 2,468 items can be brought to documented chain-of-custody status.

Tier 1 OCR-verified means the bottom-stamp text has been extracted from a photograph and confirmed against the inventory ticket. The Sternberg Collection currently has approximately 200 items at Tier 1, concentrated in anchors and high-value pieces: Giant Abbey, Doonesbury LE 10/10, Skyline GIANTS, Bahrain CMCS, Charity Edition 16/16, Architecture Series across 45+ cities.

Tier 2 photo-documented means a photograph in the archive matches the inventory line by side art, distinctive shape, or visible identifier. Tier 2 work is currently underway across approximately 700–900 items, with capacity to scale to 1,500–2,000 items.

Tier 3 inventory cross-referenced is the provenance-level verification: every inventory line linked to its box location, at least one matching photo file, an Instagram post URL where the piece appears in the @starbucks.mug.collection archive, and a series classification.

The Sternberg Collection has always been at 100% chain-of-custody. Chaim’s color-coded box system, his 9,872-photograph archive, and his 2,466-post Instagram account are the proof. The verification work now is making that chain visible and queryable for a buyer.

What a Qualified Buyer Receives

Four artifacts. Complete documentation.

“190 of 2,468 verified” sounds like 7.7%, which sounds incomplete. “Tier 1 OCR-verified for anchors, Tier 2 photo-documented for mid-tier items, Tier 3 cross-referenced for 100% of inventory” is the truth, and what it describes is complete documentation.

An auction-house specialist or museum acquisitions team performing due diligence on a $1M+ private collection sale doesn’t ask for a single verification percentage. They ask for the cross-reference table. They ask for the photo archive. They ask for the box manifest. They ask for the Instagram archive.

All four exist for the Sternberg Collection. The three-tier framework simply makes the verification work that has already been done legible to a buyer.
