In the late 1990s, Starbucks released a limited edition collector series illustrated by Garry Trudeau, the Pulitzer Prize-winning cartoonist behind Doonesbury. Ten pieces. Limited production. No reissue. The set was sold piece by piece through select Starbucks retail channels and quickly disappeared from circulation.

Today, complete intact Doonesbury LE sets are vanishingly rare. Individual pieces surface occasionally on resale markets at modest prices. The full ten-piece set, with all original pieces preserved and provenance intact, is the rarity that institutional brand-memorabilia collectors actively seek.

The Sternberg Collection holds all ten. Each piece has been photographed, catalogued, and matched to its original ticket number in the master inventory. The set has not been split. It will not be split.

A complete intact LE set is not a sum of its parts. The completion premium typically runs 2x to 3x the per-piece secondary market value — reflecting the difficulty of reassembly and the value of provenance preservation.

Why this set matters

The Doonesbury LE was Starbucks' first major licensed-illustration collaboration with a Pulitzer-winning artist. The pieces sit at the intersection of two collector universes — Starbucks brand memorabilia and Trudeau-licensed work — making complete sets attractive to a wider buyer pool than typical brand pieces.

For a museum or institutional buyer, the set represents a documented moment in late-1990s American brand-culture history: when a publicly-traded retailer commissioned a satirist whose work specifically critiqued corporate culture, and released the result as collector pieces. The cultural commentary is the artifact.

Documentation

All ten pieces are photo-verified and catalogued in the master inventory. Bottom-stamp OCR confirms ticket numbers 168501-168510. Side artwork matches Trudeau's licensed series. Each piece's storage box is mapped, color-coded, and cross-referenced to the public Instagram archive.
