A ceramic atlas of the North American city.

The Architecture Series is the largest and most geographically diverse section of the Sternberg Collection—roughly fifty pieces documenting forty-five cities through a single, consistent visual language.

Each mug carries side-art metrics: city incorporation date, elevation above sea level, land area in square miles, average annual rainfall, average daily temperature in January and July, and the city’s geographic situation. Read as a set, the Architecture Series is a quantitative atlas of late-1990s and 2000s North American urban identity, rendered in ceramic.

The series spans multiple production eras. The earliest pieces, in the 186118–186130 ticket range, date to a 2006-era release with the classic “ARCHITECTURAL SERIES” bottom stamp. A later production run uses the 455xxx ticket range with refined design language. The 18-ounce regular format carries SKU codes in the 195xxx range with original $9.95 USD retail markings; the demitasse format anchors at $7.95–$9.95 retail.

For a buyer, the Architecture Series is the workhorse of the collection: documented geographic breadth, consistent design language, multi-format completeness, and a unique cross-referential structure where each piece relates to every other through the same metric vocabulary.

Why This Matters

The Architecture Series is the connective tissue of the collection.

Where the Charity Edition tells a philanthropic story across sixteen cities and the Giants tell a flagship-city story across sixteen pieces, the Architecture Series documents Starbucks’ actual urban footprint at scale—forty-five cities, multiple formats, multiple production eras, all in the same design language.

For a brand-history archive, it is the closest thing to a complete geographic record. For a museum buyer, it offers natural display variety while maintaining series coherence. For a private collector, the breadth and ticket-level documentation make it the most defensible value-per-piece section of the collection.
