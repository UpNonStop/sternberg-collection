In the late 1990s, Starbucks released a sixteen-city Charity Edition collector series. Each mug was tied to a specific city and a specific local charitable beneficiary — environmental protection, urban beautification, literacy programs, refugee services, children's welfare. The pieces sold through their respective city Starbucks stores, with a portion of each sale donated to the named local charity.

It was a clean commercial-philanthropic exercise. Sixteen cities. Sixteen charities. Limited production. No reissue. The series predates the modern era of cause-marketing collaborations, sits in a clean cultural moment, and represents a documented chapter in late-1990s corporate-philanthropy history.

Complete intact sets are vanishingly rare. Individual Charity Edition mugs occasionally surface on resale markets at $40-$120 each, but the full sixteen-piece set with documented beneficiaries and provenance — intact, never split, never used — is a different category of object entirely.

For a buyer, the Charity Edition is one of the cleanest provenance stories in the collection: a finite, named, complete series with a clear cultural and philanthropic context. The 16-of-16 completion premium typically commands 1.5x to 2x the per-piece secondary market value.

Why this set matters

The Charity Edition documents a specific cultural moment: a publicly-traded coffee retailer, in its rapid US expansion phase of the late 1990s, formalizing relationships with named local beneficiaries in sixteen of its major US markets. Each piece carries the beneficiary information on its side art and provenance documentation, creating a built-in archive of late-1990s American urban philanthropic causes.

For a museum or institutional buyer, the set is a concrete artifact of a particular era of corporate-philanthropic practice. For a brand-history archive, it represents Starbucks' geographic expansion documented through its charitable footprint. For a private collector, it's the cleanest complete CMCS series in the collection's American subset.

The Sternberg Collection holds all sixteen. Each piece has been catalogued, photographed, and matched to its original ticket number in the master inventory. The set has never been split. It will not be split.
